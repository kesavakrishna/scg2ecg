# Calibrating to a new person (Phase 4b note)

Numbers: `results/calibration/summary.md` (generated by `scripts/calibration.py`). Every development person
is treated as an unseen person, using the Phase 3 fold models. The calibration chunk is the first 10, 30
or 60 s of the person's music record, SCG and ECG together, recorded about 35 minutes before the test data.
The test data is the same 'late' part as in every other table. U-Net rows are averaged over 3 seeds.

## Results (mean over 16 people)

| method | none | 10 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| average beat of the training people | 0.372 | | | |
| ... shifted by the delay fitted on the chunk | | 0.615 | 0.644 | 0.654 |
| average beat of the person's own chunk | | 0.822 | 0.907 | 0.913 |
| U-Net | 0.616 | | | |
| ... shifted by the delay fitted on the chunk | | 0.725 | 0.721 | 0.719 |
| ... fine-tuned on the chunk | | **0.964** | **0.973** | **0.977** |

For scale: a U-Net trained on about 35 minutes of each person's own data reaches 0.991.

- **Fine-tuning on 10 s of a new person's paired data takes the U-Net from 0.62 to 0.96.** It improves
  every one of the 16 people. The worst person goes from 0.03 to 0.90 (0.94 with 60 s). With 60 s, R-peak
  timing error drops to 2 ms and beat PPV rises from 0.93 to 0.99, so the invented beats are gone.
- **Correcting the delay alone recovers part of the gap** (0.62 to 0.72; better for 12 of 16 people,
  p = 0.003). The delay fitted on 10 s agrees with the best delay on the test data within a few ms for
  most people; subject 6 is -33 ms against -38 ms. The rest of the gap is the person's own beat shape,
  which other people's data cannot supply.
- **The person's own average beat is the right baseline for calibration, and the U-Net still beats it**
  (15 or 16 of 16 people at every length). The margin is largest with 10 s (0.96 against 0.82), where
  10 beats make a noisy average.
- The number of fine-tuning steps was chosen per fold on the 2 tune people, never on test people. The
  choice hardly matters: every option from 50 to 400 steps scores within about 0.01.

## What this does and does not show

- Calibration needs a short real ECG from the person. The use case this supports is "record the ECG
  once, then monitor with the SCG sensor alone", not "never record an ECG".
- The chunk and the test data come from the same session, 35 minutes apart, with the electrodes and SCG
  sensor never moved. CEBS has one session per person, so this cannot show that a calibration survives
  re-attaching the sensor or a new day. The Phase 5 transfer check (calibrate on one record, test on a
  separate later record) is as close as this data gets.
- With paired data from the person, the task becomes mostly same-person mapping, which was always easy
  (Phase 3: 0.99). The finding is how little of it is needed: 10 s.

## Does a calibration carry across recordings? (`results/calibration/transfer.md`)

Same test data (the late part of the music record only), 30 s of calibration from three places:

| method | start of the same record (~35 min earlier) | start of the separate after-music record |
|---|---|---|
| U-Net, shifted | 0.714 | 0.737 |
| U-Net, fine-tuned | 0.972 | **0.982** |
| person's own average beat | 0.904 | 0.875 |

- Calibrating on a different recording costs nothing for the U-Net; it is slightly better (+0.01, 11 of
  16 people). The after-music record starts right after the test minutes, so it is also closer in time.
  That probably explains the small gain: a calibration made nearer the measurement fits it better.
- Subject 1, the one person with a separate rest record (made before the music record), gets 0.928 from a
  rest-record calibration, against 0.921 from the music record's start.
- So within one session, the calibration does not depend on which recording it came from. Whether it
  survives re-attaching the sensor, or a different day, cannot be tested on CEBS.
