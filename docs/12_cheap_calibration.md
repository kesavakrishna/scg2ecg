# Cheaper calibration (follow-up 2)

Numbers: `results/cheap_calibration/summary.md` (generated by `scripts/cheap_calibration.py`). These are
development people treated as unseen people, using the Phase 3 fold models and 3 seeds. Everything was
fixed in advance: 100 fine-tuning steps, as in the holdout plan. The calibration clip comes from the start
of the person's music record; the test data is the 'late' part.

## How little ECG is enough?

| calibration | mean r | better than no calibration |
|---|---|---|
| none (U-Net) | 0.62 | |
| fine-tune on **3 s** of ECG (about 3 beats) | **0.94** | 16 / 16 |
| fine-tune on 5 s | 0.96 | 16 / 16 |
| fine-tune on 10 s | 0.97 | 16 / 16 |

Three seconds of ECG recovers 90% of what 10 s does (0.94 against 0.97; 10 s is still better for 15 of
16 people).

## Beat times only, no ECG waveform

Some devices give only the time of each heartbeat, not its shape: a smartwatch's single-lead ECG, or a
chest strap. Two ways to use those times:

| calibration from beat times | 10 s | 30 s | with 10 ms timing noise, 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| shift the output by its median offset from the given beats | 0.73 | 0.73 | 0.72 |
| fine-tune on a pseudo-target: the population average beat pasted at the given beats | 0.82 | **0.84** | 0.73 |

- **Beat times fix the timing.** The pseudo-target fine-tune brings R timing error to 2 ms and beat PPV to
  0.997, and it beats no calibration for 15 of 16 people.
- **They cannot fix the shape.** The pseudo-target has the population's beat shape, not the person's,
  so it stops at 0.84. Even 3 s of the person's real ECG beats it for 15 of 16 people (0.94).
- **It needs accurate beat times.** With 10 ms of timing noise, the pseudo-target method falls from 0.84
  to 0.73, no better than the simple shift. Beat times from an ECG are accurate to a few ms; beat times
  from a pulse sensor (PPG) are not, since pulse arrival lags the R-wave by a variable 150-300 ms. So a
  PPG watch would not do.

## Summary

The calibration needs only a few seconds, but it needs the person's real ECG shape; timing information
alone gets about halfway (0.62 → 0.84). A practical device could ask for one short ECG at set-up: a
3-5 s single-lead reading of the kind a smartwatch takes. That is a much lower bar than the 10-60 s of
Phase 4b.
