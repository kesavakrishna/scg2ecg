# Holdout results (Phase 6 note)

Numbers: `results/holdout/summary.md` (generated by `scripts/holdout.py`), scored once, following
`docs/06_holdout_plan.md`. That plan was committed before any holdout data was touched. Holdout people:
3, 9, 16, 17. Final models were trained on all 16 development people for a fixed number of epochs.

The scoring step was interrupted once by the system running out of memory, after 2 of 4 people. Nothing
had been written or looked at; the re-run is the only scoring that was seen.

## The pre-registered questions

| question | development (16 people) | holdout (4 people) | held up? |
|---|---|---|---|
| U-Net vs average-beat baseline, no calibration | U-Net 0.62, baseline 0.37 | U-Net 0.60, **baseline 0.69** (better for all 4) | **no** |
| any GAN vs U-Net | no better (within seed noise) | paired CycleGAN 0.69 and pix2pix 0.63, both above the U-Net for all 4; cGAN 0.60 | **no** (see below) |

## What held, and what did not

- **The calibration result held.** Fine-tuning on 10 s of the new person's data is the best method for
  every holdout person (0.89-0.98, mean 0.94, against 0.96 in development). Its seed spread is tiny
  (median SD 0.008). It beats the person's own average beat for all 4. Longer clips add almost nothing
  (0.93-0.94).
- **The unseen-person ranking did not hold.** In development the U-Net beat the average-beat baseline for
  14 of 16 people. On the holdout the baseline wins for all 4. The baseline happens to suit these four
  people:
  - its SCG beat detector places beats within 8 ms (24 ms in development);
  - the population average beat matches their ECG shape well (average-beat correlation 0.82, against
    0.46 in development).

  The development baseline ranged from 0.01 to 0.68 across people. Four people drawn from that range can
  easily all land high. The U-Net itself scored about as expected (0.60 against 0.62).
- **The GAN ranking did not hold either, but it is the weakest comparison.** The paired CycleGAN beat the
  U-Net for all 4 holdout people (0.69 against 0.60) after tying it in development (4 of 16). With 4
  people a 4-0 split happens by chance 1 time in 8 even when there is no difference. The development
  result (16 people, 3 seeds each, p = 0.43) is the better evidence. The honest summary: no GAN was
  reliably better.
- **One cGAN seed failed.** Trained for the pre-set 7 epochs, seed 0 of the final cGAN draws no QRS at all:
  R amplitude about 1% of real, beat sensitivity under 5%. Its r (0.24-0.68) comes from the slow waves
  alone. Seeds 1 and 2 are normal (mean about 0.67). This model starts training badly and recovers late
  (Phase 4), so a fixed epoch count is fragile for it. The pre-registered mean (0.60) includes the failed
  seed.
- **Clinical intervals:**
  - With calibration, both methods are within about 15 ms: the own average beat (QRS 1, PR 5, QT 15 ms)
    and the fine-tuned U-Net (QRS 3, PR 8, QT 15 ms).
  - Without calibration, the U-Net is at 33-38 ms and the baseline at 17-33 ms.
  - On these 4 people the own average beat is slightly better on intervals than the fine-tuned U-Net,
    while r favours the U-Net. In development they split (own beat better on QRS, U-Net on PR).

## Bottom line

- **The practical claim survives:** about 10 s of a person's own paired data turns a mediocre
  unseen-person reconstruction into a good one (0.94).
- **The comparison without calibration is fragile:** which of a learned model and a pasted average beat
  wins depends on which people you test. The development folds and the holdout disagree, and both are
  small.
- No result here supports the claim that a GAN or CycleGAN is the key ingredient.
