# Does a GAN beat the supervised model? (Phase 4 note)

Numbers: `results/gans/summary.md` (generated by `scripts/gans.py`). Setting: unseen people only. 4 folds
(train on 10 people, early-stop on 2, test on 4), 3 seeds per fold, each of the 16 development people
tested once per seed. Every GAN uses the same 1D U-Net generator, windows, optimiser and test data as
the supervised U-Net of Phase 3, so the only change is the loss.

## What was run

| name | data | generator loss |
|---|---|---|
| unet (Phase 3) | paired | L1 |
| pix2pix_adv0.1 | paired | L1 + 0.1 x LSGAN, discriminator sees SCG and ECG together |
| pix2pix_adv1 | paired | L1 + 1 x LSGAN |
| cycle_paired | paired | CycleGAN (SCG->ECG and ECG->SCG, a discriminator per domain, cycle loss) + paired L1 both ways |
| cycle_unpaired | unpaired | CycleGAN only; the ECG windows come from a separate shuffle, never the SCG's own |

Unlike the original model, the discriminators can actually discriminate (no normalisation over their
output), the cycle loss compares against the real input, and the generator output is not normalised.

## Results (mean over 16 people, seeds averaged per person)

| method | r | beats U-Net (people) | R amplitude / real | beat sensitivity | beat PPV | R timing error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| average-beat baseline | 0.372 | | 0.99 | 0.977 | 0.929 | 24 ms |
| U-Net | 0.616 | | 1.06 | 0.973 | 0.933 | 12 ms |
| pix2pix_adv0.1 | 0.617 | 7 / 16 (p = 0.53) | 1.09 | 0.980 | 0.946 | 13 ms |
| pix2pix_adv1 | 0.592 | 4 / 16 (p = 0.09) | 1.14 | 0.980 | 0.932 | 13 ms |
| cycle_paired | 0.612 | 4 / 16 (p = 0.43) | 1.09 | 0.981 | 0.957 | 14 ms |
| cycle_unpaired | 0.122 | 1 / 16 (p = 0.0006) | 0.37 | 0.897 | 0.844 | 53 ms |

- **No GAN beats the plain supervised U-Net.** The light adversarial term changes nothing; the strong
  one makes R-waves taller (12 of 16 people, p = 0.02) but the beat shape less like the real one
  (average-beat correlation lower for 13 of 16, p = 0.03) and r slightly lower. Adding the cycle loss to
  paired training changes nothing measurable.
- **Unpaired, the CycleGAN draws ECG-like beats in roughly the right places, but late.** Its r is 0.12;
  it is below the average-beat baseline for 14 of 16 people.
- Seed noise is larger than any GAN-vs-U-Net difference: the median SD of r across seeds for one person
  is 0.05-0.07 for the paired models, 0.11 unpaired.

## Why the adversarial term has nothing to do here

Trained on its own against a finished U-Net, the conditional discriminator reaches 50-58% patch accuracy
on the training people and 56-67% on new people (fold 0, one pass). On the people it trains on the
generator is already almost exact (training L1 0.06), so there is no "blurriness" for it to punish;
the errors are on new people, whom it never sees. In the full runs its loss stays near the chance value
(0.25). The weakness of SCG-to-ECG for a new person is generalisation, and an adversarial loss on the
training people does not address it.

## Much of the unseen-person error is a time shift

For each person, the constant shift of the output (within +-200 ms) that maximises r (`lags.csv`):

| method | r | r after best shift | median abs. shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| U-Net | 0.616 | 0.753 | 5 ms |
| pix2pix_adv0.1 | 0.617 | 0.750 | 5 ms |
| cycle_paired | 0.612 | 0.757 | 6 ms |
| cycle_unpaired | 0.122 | 0.654 | 47 ms |

- For most people the supervised output is within 10 ms. A few are far off, and those are the
  low scores: subject 6 is 38 ms early (r 0.03 -> 0.84 after the shift), subject 7 is 18 ms late
  (0.46 -> 0.91), subject 2 is 76 ms late (0.23 -> 0.35). A QRS lasts about 80-100 ms, so a shift of a few
  tens of ms is enough to take r to zero even when the beat shape is right. The shift is the person's own
  delay between the electrical R-wave and the main chest vibration, which ranges from 22 to 122 ms across
  people (data check) and is not something a model can read off another person's chest.
- The unpaired CycleGAN is consistently late (median 47 ms), because nothing in unpaired training ties
  the output beat to the right moment, only to roughly the right place. Subject 6, where its delay happens
  to match (10 ms), is its one good score (0.57); that is a coincidence, not skill.
- r after the best shift is an upper bound: the shift is chosen on the test data. The useful version
  is a calibration step: estimate each person's shift from a short paired recording before the test
  part, then apply it. That is the next experiment.

## Answer to the plan's question

"Does a CycleGAN beat a supervised model?" No. With paired data a correctly wired GAN, pix2pix or
CycleGAN, matches the supervised U-Net at best; without paired data the CycleGAN falls below even the
average-heartbeat baseline. The original paper's model was supervised in practice (Phase 1 note), which
fits: the supervised signal does the work. What limits SCG-to-ECG for a new person is mostly that
person's timing, not the choice of loss.

## Addendum: a cGAN with 2 generators and 6 discriminators

A paired conditional GAN with two generators (SCG to ECG, ECG to SCG) and six discriminators, three per
output domain, each seeing the input signal next to the real or generated output:

- **Waveform:** about one beat wide.
- **Coarse:** the waveform averaged down 4x, about 3.5 s wide, so it judges rhythm.
- **Spectrogram:** log-magnitude, 0-40 Hz.

The cycle and paired L1 terms are as in `cycle_paired`, and the six adversarial terms are averaged per
domain, so the total adversarial weight is the same. Same folds, seeds and test data as everything above.

| method | r | beats U-Net | r after best shift | R amplitude / real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U-Net | 0.616 | | 0.753 | 1.06 |
| cycle_paired | 0.612 | 4 / 16 (p = 0.43) | 0.757 | 1.09 |
| cgan_2g6d | 0.605 | 6 / 16 (p = 0.38) | 0.756 | 1.09 |

- **The extra discriminators change nothing measurable.** r, beat detection, timing, beat shape, time
  shift and clinical behaviour on noise all sit with the other paired models: it also draws a full-size
  ECG from white noise (`results/clinical/flatline.csv`).
- **A first version failed, and why is instructive.** With all spectrogram bins, the spectral
  discriminator told real from generated almost perfectly (score 1.09 against 0.08 after 200 steps). It
  did so from the near-empty band above the 40 Hz filter edge, which the log scale magnifies. Its gradient
  on the generator was then 23x the L1 term's. Restricting it to 0-40 Hz fixed that; the fix is in
  `scg2ecg/gans.py: SpecD`.
- **Training starts badly and recovers.** For the first 2-3 epochs r on the tune people is negative while
  the six discriminators win outright (real-vs-fake score gaps of 0.8-0.98). It then recovers, as the
  paired CycleGAN does, so it was given more patience: early stopping chose epochs 1-16.
- By the chosen epoch, the coarse and spectral heads are back near chance (score gaps of about 0.01-0.1
  in most runs). Only the beat-wide waveform head keeps a gap (0.2-0.6). There is little left for the
  extra discriminators to push on, which matches the main finding above.
- It costs about 3x the paired CycleGAN per step (131 s per quarter-size epoch with the GPU shared).
