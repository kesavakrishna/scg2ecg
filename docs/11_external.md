# External test: a second dataset (follow-up 4)

Numbers: `results/external/summary.md` (corrected) and `results/external/summary_as_planned.md` (as
committed), generated by `scripts/external.py`.

**SensSmartTech** (PhysioNet, CC BY 4.0) is a different lab, sensor and chest position from CEBS:

- 32 healthy volunteers.
- 30 s recordings with ECG lead I and one accelerometer axis perpendicular to the chest, placed between
  the V3 and V4 electrodes.
- Each person has 3+ rest recordings (one person has only 1) and 6-12 recordings after exercise, at higher
  heart rates.
- There are no beat annotations; R-peaks come from neurokit2 on the real lead I.

The CEBS models are the holdout phase's final models (trained on 16 CEBS people). Calibration uses the
person's first rest recording; scores are on their *other* recordings.

## A correction, made after the first run

The plan was committed before running. The first run capped every method near r 0.6, including the
person's own average beat pasted at the true R-peaks. The cause was the target, not the models. The raw
lead I sits on a large DC offset, and zero-phase band-pass filtering of a 30 s clip turns it into a
transient of up to 14 SD at both ends (CEBS records are minutes long, so this never showed there).

The corrected run removes each recording's median before filtering and drops 2 s at each end. Nothing else
changes, except that the longer calibration clip is now 26 s instead of 30 s. Both runs are kept; the
conclusions are the same, only the calibrated levels move.

| method | rest, as planned | rest, corrected | after exercise, corrected |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEBS U-Net, no calibration | 0.13 | 0.19 | 0.10 |
| CEBS paired CycleGAN, no calibration | 0.11 | 0.18 | 0.14 |
| average-beat baseline (CEBS population) | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.07 |
| U-Net shifted, 10 s calibration | 0.27 | 0.39 | 0.13 |
| person's own average beat, 10 s | 0.57 | 0.82 | 0.31 |
| **U-Net fine-tuned on 10 s** | 0.61 | **0.86** | 0.47 |
| U-Net fine-tuned on the whole first recording (26 s) | 0.62 | **0.90** | 0.50 |

## What it shows

- **Nothing trained on CEBS transfers to a new sensor without calibration.**
  - The U-Net, the CycleGAN and the baseline all score r 0.0-0.2 on new people with a new sensor.
  - Flipping the accelerometer's sign does not rescue them (0.21).
  - Some people's lead I points the other way. Subject 5's QRS is negative, and the CEBS model draws
    positive spikes (`results/external/examples.png`).
- **Calibration transfers.** Fine-tuning on 10 s from a different recording lifts all 31 people with a
  second rest recording, from 0.19 to 0.86. With the whole 26 s recording it reaches 0.90, beating the
  person's own average beat for 28 of 31 people. After fine-tuning, the model draws subject 5's negative
  QRS correctly.
- **Higher heart rates after exercise are the hard case.**
  - Even calibrated, r falls to about 0.5, and the higher the heart rate, the lower the r (correlation
    -0.47).
  - The calibration came from rest. Both the ECG (QT shortens, the T-wave moves) and the chest vibration
    change with heart rate.
  - A calibration at one heart rate does not cover another; a real device would need calibration at
    several heart rates, or a model that learns how beats change with rate.
- **The same pattern as CEBS, at a lower level.** CEBS gave 0.96-0.97 with 10 s of calibration. Here,
  0.86 at rest is still good: with a different sensor, placement and lab, and calibration from a
  separate recording minutes earlier.

## Limits

- 30 s recordings only; there is no long-term or between-day test here either.
- R-peaks come from a detector, not annotations. The worst person (subject 4) has a noisy reference ECG.
