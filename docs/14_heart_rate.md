# Calibrating across heart rates (A1)

Numbers: `results/heart_rate/summary.md` (generated by `scripts/heart_rate.py`).

The data is SensSmartTech, with the CEBS final U-Net, 3 seeds and 100 fine-tuning steps. Calibrated at
rest, the U-Net reached 0.86 at rest but about 0.5 after exercise (`docs/11_external.md`). Four
calibrations were compared on the same test recordings: the person's other rest recordings, and their
after-exercise recordings minus the first one, which one method uses.

| calibration | rest | after exercise | after exercise, heart rate > 1.5x the calibration's |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 s at rest | 0.86 | 0.52 | 0.31 |
| 20 s at rest (more data, same rate) | **0.89** | 0.55 | 0.31 |
| 10 s at rest + 10 s from the first after-exercise recording | 0.85 | **0.65** | **0.68** |
| 10 s at rest + the same clip time-warped to 1.2-1.6x the rate | 0.86 | 0.54 | 0.37 |

- **A real clip at a high heart rate is what helps.** Adding 10 s from right after exercise lifts
  after-exercise r by +0.12 (28 of 31 people, p < 0.001). The gain grows with the heart-rate gap: at more
  than 1.5x the calibration rate, r goes from 0.31 to 0.68. It costs a little at rest (-0.016), since half
  the calibration is now at another rate.
- **More rest data helps only at rest.** 20 s instead of 10 s improves both settings slightly (+0.02-0.03)
  but does nothing for the biggest heart-rate gaps.
- **Simulated faster beats do not substitute for real ones.** The warp keeps the QRS, shortens QT by
  sqrt(rate) and squeezes the rest of the beat. It changes nothing measurable (+0.015, p = 0.09).
  Whatever changes with heart rate in the SCG-to-ECG mapping, it is not just the timing of the waves: the
  chest vibration itself changes with how hard the heart works.
- **Practical upshot:** calibrate twice, at rest and after a minute of exercise (10 s each). That keeps
  quality up across the heart rates of everyday life. Rates far beyond both clips are untested.
